Recycling Guide

What goes where, at a glance — so nothing ends up in the wrong bin.

Why it matters

Recyclables put in the wrong bin, or contaminated with food and liquid, often end up incinerated instead of recycled. A little sorting at your end goes a long way — here's a quick guide to what belongs in each of Singapore's recycling streams.

♻️

Blue Bins Paper · Plastic · Glass · Metal

Recycle these

  • Newspapers, magazines, flattened cardboard
  • Plastic bottles & containers, rinsed
  • Glass bottles & jars
  • Metal cans and empty aerosol cans
  • Drink cartons (e.g. milk, juice)

Not these

  • Food waste or greasy/soiled paper (e.g. pizza boxes)
  • Plastic bags, cling wrap, styrofoam
  • Broken glass, mirrors, ceramics
  • Electronics, batteries, light bulbs
  • Clothing or other textiles
🔌

E-Waste Electronics & batteries

Recycle these

  • Mobile phones, laptops, tablets
  • Chargers, cables, power banks
  • Batteries (tape the terminals first)
  • Small appliances — kettles, hairdryers, toasters
  • Light bulbs and fluorescent tubes

Not these

  • Large appliances (fridges, washing machines) — these need a separate bulky e-waste collection
  • Items with cracked casings or leaking batteries — handle separately, don't drop in the bin
  • Non-electronic items
👕

Textile Bins Clothes & fabric

Recycle these

  • Clean, dry clothing of any condition
  • Bags, belts, and paired shoes
  • Bed linens, curtains, towels

Not these

  • Wet, soiled, or moldy fabric
  • Textiles contaminated with paint or chemicals
  • Single/unpaired shoes
🥤

Beverage Containers BCRS return points

Recycle these

  • Empty PET plastic drink bottles
  • Empty aluminium/metal drink cans
  • Containers with the deposit refund logo

Not these

  • Glass beverage bottles
  • Non-beverage containers
  • Crushed containers with an unreadable barcode

General tips

  • Rinse food and drink containers before recycling — no need to remove labels.
  • Flatten cardboard boxes to save space in the bin.
  • Don't bag your recyclables in plastic bags — put items in loose.
  • When in doubt, leave it out — one contaminated item can spoil a whole load.
